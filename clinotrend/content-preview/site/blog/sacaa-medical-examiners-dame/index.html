SACAA medical examiners: how to find and check a designated examiner (DAME)

What a DAME is, Regular versus Senior, how to check an examiner on the SACAA list before you book, and why it matters.

By Dr A.O. Ndhlovu, MBChB (UCT), DOHM (UP), Cert. Avmed (SMU), SACAA Designated Aviation Medical Examiner (MR 336)

Draft prepared 29 October 2026Last updated 29 October 20263 min readFor review by Dr A.O. Ndhlovu

Quick answer

Aviation medicals for Class 1, 2 and 3 must be done by a SACAA Designated Aviation Medical Examiner (DAME). SACAA publishes lists of Regular DAMEs (Class II and IV) and Senior DAMEs (Class I to IV) on its aviation medicine page. Check that your examiner is on the current list, and for the right class, before you book.

Your medical certificate is only as good as the examiner who issued it. This guide explains what a Designated Aviation Medical Examiner is, which examiner can do which class, and how to check an examiner yourself in a couple of minutes.

What is a DAME?

A DAME is a doctor designated by the Director of Civil Aviation to do the medical examinations and tests needed for SACAA medical certificates. Regulation 67.00.4 of Part 67 says the Director signs and issues each DAME a document that names the examiner and states that he or she may examine applicants, issue the certificate, or defer it until SACAA gives an instruction.

People often search for a "SACAA approved" or "SACAA accredited" doctor. The term the regulations use is designated.

Regular DAME or Senior DAME: what is the difference?

SACAA examiner categories
ExaminerClassesTypical licences
Regular DAMEClass II and Class IVStudent and private pilots, cabin crew, recreational pilots
Senior DAMEClass I, II, III and IVAlso commercial and airline pilots and air traffic services

A Class 4 medical is the exception to the DAME rule: regulation 67.00.5 lets any doctor registered with the HPCSA do the examination, with the application and results sent to SACAA for verification within 60 days. Not sure which class you need? See SACAA medical classes explained.

How do I check that an examiner is designated?

  1. Open the SACAA aviation medicine page and find the lists of Regular and Senior Designated Aviation Medical Examiners.
  2. Search the list for the doctor's name. Each entry shows a designation code, the practice address, the area and contact details.
  3. Check the category: a Regular DAME is listed for Class II and IV only.
  4. At your appointment you can ask to see the designation certificate. SACAA procedure AM007 says a DAME must have proof of designation, a certificate showing its period of validity, at the practice.

Why it matters

A designation has a validity period and can lapse. In 2024 SACAA published a notice about certificates issued by an examiner whose designation had lapsed, and asked the licence holders concerned to be examined again because those certificates were not valid. A quick check before you book avoids that risk.

Questions and answers

What does DAME stand for?

+

Designated Aviation Medical Examiner: a doctor designated by the Director of Civil Aviation under Part 67, regulation 67.00.4, to do aviation medical examinations and issue or defer SACAA medical certificates.

Where is the list of SACAA medical examiners?

+

On the SACAA aviation medicine page, which links to the lists of Regular and Senior Designated Aviation Medical Examiners with their codes, areas and contact numbers.

Can a Regular DAME renew my Class 2?

+

Yes. Regular DAMEs examine for and issue Class II and Class IV certificates. Class I and Class III need a Senior DAME.

Can my own GP do my aviation medical?

+

For Class 1, 2 and 3, only if your GP is a SACAA designated examiner for that class. For a Class 4, any HPCSA registered doctor may do the examination.
