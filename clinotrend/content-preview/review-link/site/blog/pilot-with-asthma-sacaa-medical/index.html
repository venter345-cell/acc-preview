Can you be a pilot with asthma? What the SACAA medical looks at

How asthma is handled at a SACAA Class 2 or Class 4 medical: what to declare, which tests and reports the SACAA protocol lists, and how student pilots can plan ahead. General information, not personal advice.

By Dr A.O. Ndhlovu, MBChB (UCT), DOHM (UP), Cert. Avmed (SMU), SACAA Designated Aviation Medical Examiner (MR 336)

Published 19 November 2026Last updated 19 November 20267 min read

Quick answer

Asthma is not an automatic no at a SACAA medical, and not an automatic yes. Declare it, even if it was only in childhood, and list every inhaler and medicine. The SACAA asthma protocol lists lung function tests, a chest X-ray at the initial examination and, in some cases, a specialist report. The examiner then decides.

  • Form CA 67-02(a) asks whether you have, or have ever had, "asthma, lung disease", and asks for every medicine, including non-prescribed medicine.
  • The summarised SACAA medical protocols (item 29, asthma) list lung function tests, a chest X-ray on initial examination, and a pulmonologist's report if the FEV1/FVC ratio is 70% or lower.
  • Student pilots need a Class 2 for the SPL and PPL. Aviation medicals at ClinoTrend start from R1,500.

This is general information, not individual medical advice, and it does not promise fitness. Asthma is one of the most common questions from people thinking about flight training, often because they had it as a child. This guide explains what the official SACAA documents say, what to bring, and how to plan a first Class 2 medical or a Class 4 medical if asthma is part of your history.

Can you be a pilot if you have asthma?

Asthma is not on a list of automatic bars, and a diagnosis on its own does not decide the outcome. The SACAA Aviation Medicine page explains that aviation authorities permit people with certain medical conditions to be certified, provided the condition does not compromise aviation safety. What matters is how the condition behaves now: how well it is controlled, how your lungs perform on testing, and which medicines you use.

The medical standards themselves sit in Document SA-CATS 67, under regulation 67.00.2(6) of Part 67. The examiner applies those standards and the SACAA protocols to your case. A blog post cannot do that for you, and nobody can promise a certificate before the examination.

Do I have to declare childhood asthma?

Yes. The SACAA Aviation Medical Report, form CA 67-02(a), asks "Do you have, or have you ever had" a list of conditions, and one of them is asthma, lung disease. That covers asthma that settled years ago as well as asthma you treat today. The same form asks about tobacco use, and asks for every medicine you currently use, including non-prescribed medicine, with the name, dose, date started and the reason.

Leaving out a past diagnosis does not make it go away, and incomplete disclosure is a problem in itself. The ClinoTrend online SACAA application form asks the same history questions before your visit, so you can think about dates and details in advance.

What tests does the SACAA asthma protocol list?

The SACAA Summarised Medical Protocols (effective 16 November 2021) include an asthma entry, item 29. It lists:

  • Lung function tests (spirometry).
  • A chest X-ray, PA and lateral, on the initial examination, and later chest X-rays only on indication.
  • A pulmonologist's report if the FEV1/FVC ratio is 70% or lower.

Form CA 67-02(a) has fields for a lung function test and a chest X-ray among its special investigations. The doctor tells you which of these apply to you. If a lung function test (spirometry) is needed, ClinoTrend can do it at the practice during the medical: you blow out and breathe in so the examiner can measure how your lungs are working. If a chest X-ray is needed, it can be done at a nearby radiology practice, and you bring the report to your appointment. See what is included in a SACAA aviation medical for the tests done at every visit.

Is it different for a Class 4 medical or cabin crew?

Class 4 covers cabin crew and recreational licences such as microlight, gyroplane, light sport and glider pilots. A SACAA workshop presentation on Class 4 medical standards (April 2024) lists the points looked at for an applicant with asthma:

  • lung function results within the Class 4 values
  • a favourable report from the physician or examiner
  • no bronchospasm or associated respiratory infection
  • no emergency treatment for asthma in the last 3 months
  • treatment limited to acceptable medication

Regulation 67.00.2(7) adds one more rule: a Class 4 holder who flies above 12 000 ft must meet the respiratory and lung function test requirements of a Class 2 certificate. Cabin crew can read more on the cabin crew medical page.

Can I fly if I use an inhaler?

Some asthma medicines are acceptable in aviation and some are not, and the timing of some medicines before flight can matter. This guide does not publish a list of named inhalers, because an official SACAA list that covers every product was not available to quote in full. What you should do is simple: bring the names and doses of every inhaler and tablet you use, or the boxes, and tell the examiner how often you use your reliever.

Do not stop or change an asthma medicine to "pass" a medical. That is a decision for your treating doctor, and the examiner needs to see your usual treatment.

What should a student pilot with asthma do first?

Under Part 61, a student pilot licence needs a valid medical certificate before you fly solo, and the SPL and PPL sit under Class 2. If asthma is part of your history, book the first medical early, ideally before you pay for a full course. That leaves time for a lung function test, a chest X-ray or a letter from your own doctor if the examiner asks for one. Our student pilot medical guide and the student pilot medical page cover the rest of the first-medical steps.

A useful letter from your treating doctor describes the diagnosis, how often you have symptoms, any hospital or emergency visits, your current treatment, and recent lung function results if you have them. It does not replace the aviation medical.

If you plan a commercial career, the commercial pilot licence, airline transport pilot licence and instrument rating need a Class 1 medical, which only a SACAA Senior DAME can issue. ClinoTrend does not issue Class 1. If asthma is part of your history, you may want to ask a Senior DAME about Class 1 early. See SACAA Class 1 medicals and Senior DAMEs.

Flight schools can arrange group medicals for students at Grand Central Airport, Midrand. See the flight schools page.

What if my asthma flares up between medicals?

Regulation 67.00.9 says a certificate holder must not act as a crew member while aware of a medical condition or medication that could affect the validity of the certificate. It also requires the holder to notify the designated body of any hospitalisation or regular use of medication that leaves the holder unable to meet the medical standards. If you have a significant flare-up, a hospital visit or a new regular medicine, stop and speak to your examiner before you fly again.

What happens if more information is needed?

If the examiner needs a test result or a report, the usual step is a deferral, not a refusal. SACAA procedure AM007 says complicated cases are referred to the Aeromedical Committee. See what happens if an aviation medical is deferred and what can defer or limit a SACAA medical.

Questions and answers

Can you be a pilot with asthma in South Africa?

+

It is possible for some people, depending on how the asthma is controlled, lung function results and medicines. It is not automatic either way. The examiner applies the SACAA standards to your case.

I had asthma as a child. Do I have to declare it?

+

Yes. Form CA 67-02(a) asks whether you have, or have ever had, asthma or lung disease. Declare it and give the dates you remember.

Will I need a lung function test?

+

The SACAA asthma protocol lists lung function tests. The doctor tells you if one is needed. If it is, ClinoTrend can do the test at the practice during the medical: you blow out and breathe in so the examiner can measure how your lungs are working.

Do I need a chest X-ray if I have asthma?

+

The protocol lists a chest X-ray on the initial examination, and later only on indication. The doctor tells you if you need one.

Can I use my inhaler and still fly?

+

Some asthma medicines are acceptable and some are not. Bring the names and doses of everything you use and do not stop a medicine without your own doctor.

Can I become a commercial pilot with asthma?

+

A commercial licence needs a Class 1 medical from a Senior DAME, who assesses asthma against the Class 1 standards. ClinoTrend does Class 2 and Class 4 only.

Can cabin crew have asthma?

+

Cabin crew need a Class 4. The SACAA Class 4 standards look at lung function, control, recent emergency treatment and medication.
