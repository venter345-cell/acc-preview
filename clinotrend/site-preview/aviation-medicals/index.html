Class II and Class IV aviation medicals in Midrand

For student and private pilots, cabin crew and recreational pilots, with Dr A.O. Ndhlovu at Gate 2, Office TG6B. Drive in, or fly in to FAGC and park on the apron outside the terminal doors.

How long is my medical valid?

Which medical do you need?

ClassWho it is for
Class IIStudent pilots (SPL) and private pilots (PPL) for aeroplane, airship and helicopter, plus other licences listed by SACAA.
Class IVCabin crew, and recreational pilots such as microlight, glider, gyroplane and free balloon pilots.

Class I (commercial and airline pilots) and Class III (air traffic controllers) need a SACAA Senior DAME and are not done here. Sources: SACAA classes of medical certificates and SACAA frequently asked questions.

How the medical works

  1. 01

    Book

    Book online or contact us. Tell us the class you need, whether this is your first medical or a renewal, and about any medical condition or regular medicine.

  2. 02

    Prepare

    Gather the documents in the checklist below. Rest well, and avoid caffeine, tobacco and high sugar meals beforehand, as they can affect urine and ECG results. Arrive 10 minutes early.

  3. 03

    Examination

    Dr A.O. Ndhlovu takes your history and examines you. A resting ECG, vision testing, a hearing test (audiometry) and urine screening are done on site. Allow 30 to 60 minutes.

  4. 04

    Outcome

    In most cases your aviation medical is completed on the same day, depending on your case. If you meet the SACAA standards and no further reports are needed, the certificate is issued at the end of the examination. Otherwise it is deferred until the extra information arrives, and we explain what is needed.

What to bring

  • Your South African ID or passport
  • Any aviation licence you hold, and your logbook if you are renewing
  • Your most recent aviation medical certificate, if you have one
  • A list of every medicine you take, including over the counter medicines
  • Glasses, contact lenses or hearing aids, if you use them
  • Recent reports from your doctor or specialist if you have a medical condition
  • Your medical aid card, if applicable

The SACAA form: CA 67-02(a)

The Aviation Medical Report is the form used on the day. Read it beforehand to see the history questions. Declare all medicines and healthcare visits it asks about; for unchanged conditions you may write "Previously reported, no change".

Aviation medical questions

Where can I get a SACAA Class 2 medical in Midrand?

+

At Clinotrend Health & Aviation, Main Terminal Building, Gate 2, Office TG6B, Grand Central Airport, 3 New Road, Midrand, 1685, with Dr A.O. Ndhlovu, a SACAA Regular Designated Aviation Medical Examiner (MR 336) who does Class II and Class IV medicals.

Which class do cabin crew need?

+

Class 4. SACAA's frequently asked questions state that cabin crew have required a Class 4 medical since the regulation was promulgated in May 2019. Some SACAA pages still list cabin crew under Class II, so tell us you are cabin crew when you book.

Can my own GP do a Class 4 medical?

+

SACAA allows a Class 4 applicant to be examined by a general practitioner, provided the examination follows Part 67 and the results are sent on the prescribed form for verification. Class 1, 2 and 3 applicants must see a designated aviation medical examiner.

How much does an aviation medical cost?

+

Fees are not published on the website. We tell you the fee when you book. It depends on the class, on whether it is a first medical or a renewal, and on any extra tests or reports.

What if my certificate is about to expire?

+

Book your renewal a few weeks before the expiry date. If you need an extension, SACAA procedure AM007 says you must contact the SACAA medical department before the certificate expires.
