Student pilots

Student pilot medical in South Africa: what you need before you fly solo

Which medical a student pilot needs, when to book it, what the first examination involves and what to bring. For new students, parents and flight schools.

By Dr A.O. Ndhlovu, MBChB (UCT), DOHM (UP), Cert. Avmed (SMU), SACAA Designated Aviation Medical Examiner (MR 336)

Draft prepared 29 October 2026Last updated 29 October 20264 min readFor review by Dr A.O. Ndhlovu

Quick answer

To be issued a student pilot licence (SPL) you must be at least 15, be registered with an approved training organisation (ATO) and hold a valid Part 67 medical certificate. For an aeroplane or helicopter SPL that is a Class 2 medical, done by a SACAA designated examiner. Book it early in your training, before you need your SPL.

A first aviation medical is one of the early steps in learning to fly. It is also a step that can hold up training if it is left late. This guide is for new student pilots, their parents and the flight schools that train them. It explains which certificate you need, when to get it and what happens at the first examination.

Do student pilots need a medical?

Yes. Part 61, regulation 61.02.1 (as amended on 21 August 2026), says an applicant for a student pilot licence (SPL) or integrated student pilot licence (ISPL) must be 15 years or older, hold a valid medical certificate issued in terms of Part 67, and be registered with an approved training organisation (ATO). Your SPL is what allows you to fly solo under instruction, so the medical comes first.

Which medical class does a student pilot need?

Regulation 67.00.2 lists the SPL under Class 2, alongside the private pilot licence (PPL). Students on recreational licences are different: an SPL for a microlight, gyroplane, light sport aeroplane or glider licence sits under Class 4. Our guide to SACAA medical classes has the full list.

Planning to fly commercially?

A commercial pilot licence (CPL), an airline transport pilot licence (ATPL) and an instrument rating all need a Class 1 medical, which only a SACAA Senior DAME may do. Ask your flight school when it wants you to hold a Class 1. Dr Ndhlovu is a Regular DAME, so at ClinoTrend we do the Class 2 that covers your SPL and PPL, not the Class 1.

When should I book my first medical?

Book it before your first solo is close, ideally when you sign up with your flight school. A first medical sometimes needs an extra report, for example from an optometrist, or a test that is not done on the day. Booking early leaves time for that without holding up your training. Your flight school will tell you when it needs to see your certificate.

What happens at a first Class 2 medical?

You complete your medical history on the SACAA form and sign a declaration that it is accurate, complete and true, as regulation 67.00.7 requires. The doctor then examines you. At ClinoTrend these tests are done on site:

  • Resting ECG
  • Vision testing
  • Hearing test (audiometry)
  • Urine screening

SACAA's medical requirements list a chest X-ray at the initial examination, and afterwards only when it is indicated on clinical grounds. If your first medical needs anything that is not done on site, we tell you when you book. Allow about 30 to 60 minutes.

What should a student pilot bring?

  • Your South African ID or passport
  • Glasses or contact lenses, and a recent optometrist prescription if you have one
  • A list of every medicine you take, including over the counter medicines and supplements
  • Reports from your doctor or specialist for any medical condition, past operation or hospital stay
  • Any letter or form from your flight school

You can read the SACAA Aviation Medical Report form, CA 67-02(a).pdf) beforehand to see the history questions. Be complete and honest. If something needs a report, it is much easier to sort out before the examination than after.

Under 18?

Students can start from 15. If you are under 18, a parent or guardian is welcome to come with you and can help with your medical history.

How long is a student pilot's Class 2 valid?

It depends on your age, and the certificate runs to the last day of a month. Most student pilots are under 40. See How long is a Class 2 medical valid? for the periods SACAA publishes and a calculator to work out your date.

Questions and answers

Do I need a medical before my first flying lesson?

+

You need a valid Part 67 medical certificate to be issued a student pilot licence, which you need before you fly solo. Ask your flight school when it wants to see it; many students book the medical when they sign up.

What medical class is a student pilot licence?

+

Class 2 for the aeroplane, airship and helicopter SPL. A student licence for a microlight, gyroplane, light sport aeroplane or glider sits under Class 4.

How old must I be for a student pilot licence?

+

15 or older, according to Part 61, regulation 61.02.1.

Can I do my Class 1 medical at ClinoTrend?

+

No. A Class 1 needs a SACAA Senior DAME. Dr Ndhlovu is a Regular DAME and does Class 2 and Class 4 medicals.

Can a flight school book medicals for a group of students?

+

Yes. Group bookings for flight schools are arranged in advance. Call 011 568 7979 with the number of students and the dates that suit you.
