Validity

How long a Class 2 medical is valid, by age

Class 2 validity under Part 67: 60 months under 40, 24 months after 40, 12 months after 50. Class 4 periods, how the date is counted, and a calculator.

By Dr A.O. Ndhlovu, MBChB (UCT), DOHM (UP), Cert. Avmed (SMU), SACAA Designated Aviation Medical Examiner (MR 336)

Published 10 November 2026Last updated 10 November 20264 min read

Quick answer

Under Part 67, regulation 67.00.6(3), a Class 2 medical is valid for 60 months if you are less than 40, 24 months after your 40th birthday, and 12 months after your 50th birthday, running to the last day of a month. Older FAQ pages show shorter periods; this practice follows the regulation. The date on your certificate counts.

  • A Class 4 certificate is valid for 60 months if you are less than 40, and 36 months from age 40, under regulation 67.00.6(4).
  • A DAME or a SACAA medical assessor may shorten the period and endorse a limitation, under regulation 67.00.6(5).
  • Unused time is not carried over when you renew: the new certificate runs from the month it is issued.

The date on your medical certificate is the date that matters. The periods below come from Part 67, regulation 67.00.6, and from the SACAA summarised medical protocols (effective 16 November 2021). Older SACAA FAQ pages show shorter Class 2 periods. This practice follows the regulation.

How long is a Class 2 medical valid?

Regulation 67.00.6(3) says a Class 2 certificate is valid for:

  • 60 months, if the holder is less than 40 years of age
  • 24 months, once the holder has passed their 40th birthday
  • 12 months, once the holder has passed their 50th birthday

The period is calculated from the last day of the calendar month in which the certificate is issued. For example, a Class 2 issued on 10 March 2026 to a holder younger than 40 expires on 31 March 2031. The same issue date expires on 31 March 2028 after the 40th birthday, and on 31 March 2027 after the 50th birthday.

How is the expiry date counted?

Validity always runs to the last day of a month. If a certificate is issued part way through a month, that whole month is counted, and the period then runs in calendar months from the end of that month. Medical conditions can mean more frequent examinations, so always use the date printed on your certificate.

How long is a Class 4 medical valid?

Regulation 67.00.6(4) says a Class 4 certificate is issued for a period not exceeding 60 calendar months if the applicant is less than 40 years of age, and 36 calendar months if the applicant is 40 years of age or more, again to the last day of the month. Cabin crew may be issued Class 2 or Class 4; use the periods that match the class on your certificate. See cabin crew medical requirements.

What about Class 1 and Class 3?

Class 1 periods are set out in regulation 67.00.6(1). In short, a Class 1 certificate is issued for 12 months if the applicant is less than 40. From age 40 the period depends on the licence and the type of operation: six months for an airline transport pilot in single-crew commercial air transport operations; 12 months for an airline transport pilot in multi-crew operations, and for a commercial pilot, until age 60; and six months from age 60 for those last two groups. Holders in the 12 month bands from age 40 may also have extra reporting duties under regulation 67.00.6(2).

Class 3, under regulation 67.00.6(3), is 48 months if the holder is less than 40, 24 months after the 40th birthday, and 12 months after the 50th birthday. Class 1 and Class 3 medicals need a SACAA Senior DAME. ClinoTrend does not currently issue Class 1 or Class 3.

Can the period be shorter?

Yes. Regulation 67.00.6(5) allows a DAME or a medical assessor to reduce the period of validity and endorse the reason, or any limitation, if a further examination or test is needed, or if safe performance of the holder's duties depends on a shorter period or a special limitation.

What changes when I turn 40 or 50?

The Class 2 period is set by your age on the date the certificate is issued. Once you have passed your 40th birthday, a new Class 2 is issued for 24 months. Once you have passed your 50th birthday, a new Class 2 is issued for 12 months. Turning 40 or 50 during a current period does not cut that certificate short; the shorter period applies at the next issue.

How should I plan renewals?

Book a few weeks before the expiry date, so there is time if the examiner asks for a specialist letter or an extra test. Renewing many months early does not add the unused time to the new certificate, because the new period starts from the month of issue. Our Class 2 renewal guide walks through the steps.

What if I am close to expiry?

Regulation 67.00.6(6) says you must apply for an extension at least 15 days before your certificate expires. The Director may extend it, on conditions, for no more than 30 days. SACAA procedure AM007 also says you must contact the SACAA medical department before the certificate expires. An extension is a safety net, not a plan.

+

60 months if you are less than 40 years of age, and 36 months if you are 40 or older, under regulation 67.00.6(4), to the last day of a month.

Can my certificate be valid for a shorter time?

+

Yes. Regulation 67.00.6(5) allows a DAME or a medical assessor to reduce the period and endorse a reason or a limitation when a further test is needed, or when safety depends on it.

Can I extend my medical certificate?

+

Yes, by applying to SACAA at least 15 days before expiry. Regulation 67.00.6(6) allows an extension of no more than 30 days, on conditions. Contact the SACAA medical department before the certificate expires.
