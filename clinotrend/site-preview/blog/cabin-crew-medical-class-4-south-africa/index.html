Cabin crew

Cabin crew medical in South Africa: the Class 4 requirements explained

Which SACAA medical class cabin crew need, who can examine you, what is tested, how long it lasts and what to bring.

Draft prepared 23 October 20265 min readClinotrend Health & Aviation, for review by Dr A.O. Ndhlovu

Draft, for review. This post is a draft for Dr Ndhlovu to check before it is published. See the growth plan

Aviation medicals are not only for pilots. If you work as cabin crew in South Africa, or you are about to start a cabin crew course, you need a valid SACAA medical certificate too. This guide explains which class applies, who can examine you and what to expect on the day.

Which medical class do cabin crew need?

Part 64 of the Civil Aviation Regulations, which covers cabin crew licensing, says in regulation 64.01.6 that an applicant for or holder of a cabin crew member licence must obtain a medical certificate issued in terms of Part 67. That wording was substituted in March 2019.

The amended Part 67 regulation 67.00.2 then lists the cabin crew member licence under Class 4, alongside recreational licences such as microlight, gyroplane and glider. SACAA's frequently asked questions confirm it: the regulation was promulgated in May 2019, cabin crew now require a Class 4 medical, and a DAME should issue a Class 4 certificate.

You may still see cabin crew listed under Class II on SACAA's aviation medicine web page, which predates the change. If an employer or training school asks for a specific class, follow their instruction and tell us when you book, so we issue the right certificate.

Who can do a cabin crew medical?

Class 4 is the one class that does not have to be done by a designated examiner. Regulation 67.00.5 allows any medical practitioner registered with the HPCSA to perform the examination, provided the application, supporting reports and test results are sent to the designated body within 60 days for verification before the certificate is issued.

A SACAA Designated Aviation Medical Examiner (DAME) can also examine you and issue the certificate. Regular and Senior DAMEs are both authorised for Class IV. Dr A.O. Ndhlovu at Clinotrend is a Regular DAME (SACAA MR 336), so a cabin crew Class 4 medical is within his scope, at Grand Central Airport in Midrand.

What does the cabin crew medical test?

The medical checks that you can safely do the duties of cabin crew, including in an emergency. At Clinotrend it includes:

  • Your medical history, taken on the SACAA form CA 67-02(a), and a signed declaration
  • A physical examination
  • A resting ECG
  • Vision testing (bring your glasses or contact lenses)
  • A hearing test (audiometry)
  • Urine screening

Depending on your history, the examiner may ask for more information. The SACAA medical protocols in line with Part 67 describe how some conditions are assessed for cabin crew case by case with a specialist report, for example migraine, a single seizure or a moderate head injury. If you have a condition like this, contact us before your medical so you can bring the right reports and avoid a second visit.

Medical standards versus airline requirements

Cabin crew adverts often list height or arm reach ranges, swimming ability, grooming standards and a minimum school qualification. Those are airline or training school requirements, not Part 67 medical standards. The medical certificate covers your medical fitness only. Check the advert or ask the school for its other requirements.

How long is a cabin crew medical valid?

SACAA's frequently asked questions list these periods for a Class 4 certificate, which runs to the last day of a month:

SACAA notes that the frequency of examinations may also vary depending on medical conditions. See How long is a Class 2 medical valid? for Class 2 periods and an expiry calculator.

Starting a cabin crew course?

Because Part 64 requires an applicant for a cabin crew licence to hold a Part 67 medical certificate, you need it before you apply for your licence. Many training schools ask for it at the start of the course, so ask your school when they want it. Getting it done early also tells you about anything that needs attention, such as an eyesight prescription, before you have paid course fees.

What to bring

  • Your South African ID or passport
  • Your cabin crew licence and your current medical certificate, if you have them
  • Your glasses or contact lenses, and your hearing aids if you use them
  • A list of the medicines you take, including over the counter medicines
  • Reports from your doctor or specialist for any medical condition
  • Any form or letter from your employer or training school

Questions and answers

Do cabin crew need a Class 2 or a Class 4 medical?

+

Class 4. The amended Part 67 regulation 67.00.2 lists the cabin crew member licence under Class 4, and SACAA confirms that cabin crew have needed a Class 4 medical since May 2019.

Can my own GP do my cabin crew medical?

+

Yes, if he or she is registered with the HPCSA and follows the Part 67 requirements, sending the application and results for verification within 60 days. A SACAA designated examiner, such as Dr Ndhlovu (MR 336), can also do it.

How long is a cabin crew medical valid?

+

SACAA lists 60 months under 40 and 36 months over 40 for a Class 4 certificate. Medical conditions can mean more frequent examinations.

Do I need the medical before my cabin crew course?

+

You need it before you apply for your cabin crew licence. Many schools ask for it at the start of the course, so check with your school.

Are height and swimming part of the medical?

+

No. Height, reach and swimming requirements in cabin crew adverts are set by airlines and schools. The medical certificate covers medical fitness under Part 67.
