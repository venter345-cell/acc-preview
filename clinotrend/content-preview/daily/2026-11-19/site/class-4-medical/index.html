Class 4 aviation medical certificate for cabin crew and recreational pilots

Dr A.O. Ndhlovu is a SACAA Regular Designated Aviation Medical Examiner (DAME, MR 336). He issues Class 4 medicals for cabin crew and recreational pilots, and Class 2 medicals where that is what is required, at ClinoTrend Health & Aviation, Gate 2, Office TG6B, Grand Central Airport, Midrand. He does not issue Class 1 medicals.

Who needs a Class 4 medical?

Under amended Part 67, regulation 67.00.2, a Class 4 aviation medical certificate is listed for:

  • The cabin crew member licence
  • Recreational licences such as microlight, gyroplane, light sport aeroplane, non-commercial glider and non-commercial free balloon, and other licences on the SACAA classes list

Some recreational licences, including paragliding, hang gliding and parachute licences, use a Class 5 medical instead. Under regulation 67.00.2(8) and (9), the Class 5 declaration has to be signed by a medical practitioner registered under the Health Professions Act. Dr A.O. Ndhlovu issues Class 2 and Class 4 medicals. He does not issue Class 1.

More detail: cabin crew medical and recreational pilot medical.

Source: SACAA classes of medical certificates (Part 67, regulation 67.00.2).

How long a Class 4 certificate is valid

CertificateUnder 4040 or older
Class 460 months36 months
Class 260 months24 months, then 12 months from 50

These periods follow Part 67 regulation 67.00.6. A Class 4 certificate is issued for a period not exceeding 60 calendar months if you are under 40 on the date it is issued, and 36 calendar months if you are 40 or older, calculated from the last day of the calendar month of issue. A Class 2 certificate, if that is what your operator asks for, is valid for 60 months if you are under 40, 24 months from 40, and 12 months from 50. A DAME may reduce the period if a medical reason requires it. The date printed on your certificate is the one that applies.

Source: Civil Aviation Regulations, Part 67, regulation 67.00.6.

The yearly Class 4 declaration

If you hold a Class 4 medical certificate, regulation 67.00.9(3) requires you to complete and submit a medical declaration to the designated body every year, on the form prescribed in SA-CATS 67. This is in addition to renewing the certificate when it expires. Contact the practice or SACAA if you are unsure which form to use.

Source: Civil Aviation Regulations, Part 67, regulation 67.00.9.

Class 2 for cabin crew, and Class 1

Some SACAA pages still list cabin crew under Class 2 or Class II, and some airlines, operators or academies still ask for a Class 2 medical. Dr A.O. Ndhlovu issues both Class 2 and Class 4, so book the class you have been asked for. If you are unsure, ask them before you book.

Class 1 medicals (commercial and airline transport pilots) and Class 3 medicals (air traffic controllers) need a SACAA Senior DAME. ClinoTrend does not currently issue Class 1 or Class 3.

What the medical includes

Dr A.O. Ndhlovu takes your medical history on the SACAA form CA 67-02(a) and examines you. A resting ECG, vision testing, a hearing test (audiometry) and urine screening are done on site. The doctor tells you if a further test or report is needed. If you meet the SACAA standards and no further reports are needed, the certificate is issued at the end of the examination.

Complete the SACAA application form online before you arrive. Aviation medicals are by appointment only. Ask the practice how long to allow when you book.

What to bring

  • Your South African ID or passport
  • Your cabin crew or recreational licence, if you already hold one
  • Your most recent aviation medical certificate, if you have one
  • A list of every medicine you take, including over the counter medicines
  • Glasses, contact lenses or hearing aids, if you use them
  • Recent reports from your doctor or specialist if you have a medical condition
  • Your medical aid card, if applicable

Fee and booking

Aviation medicals start from R1,500. Extra tests or reports are added only if the doctor finds they are needed during the medical. We confirm the fee when you book.

Paying: Payment by EFT or card is required at the time of your visit. We accept most medical aids and charge medical aid rates. Self-pay patients receive a quote before treatment.

Class 4 medical questions

What is a Class 4 aviation medical certificate?

+

A Class 4 medical certificate is issued under Part 67 of the Civil Aviation Regulations. Amended Part 67 lists it for the cabin crew member licence and for several recreational licences, including microlight, gyroplane, light sport aeroplane, non-commercial glider and non-commercial free balloon. Dr A.O. Ndhlovu issues Class 4 medicals at Grand Central Airport, Midrand.

How long is a Class 4 medical valid?

+

Under regulation 67.00.6(4), a Class 4 certificate is issued for a period not exceeding 60 calendar months if you are under 40 on the date it is issued, and 36 calendar months if you are 40 or older, calculated from the last day of the calendar month of issue. A DAME may shorten the period if a medical reason requires it. The date on your certificate is the one that applies.

Do cabin crew need a Class 4 or a Class 2 medical?

+

Amended Part 67 lists the cabin crew member licence under Class 4. Some SACAA pages still list Class 2 or Class II, and some airlines or operators still ask for Class 2. ClinoTrend can issue Class 2 or Class 4 for cabin crew. Book the class your airline, operator or academy asks for.

Does ClinoTrend issue Class 1 medicals?

+

No. Class 1 and Class 3 medicals need a SACAA Senior DAME. Dr Ndhlovu is a Regular DAME and issues Class 2 and Class 4 medicals only.

How much does a Class 4 medical cost?

+

Aviation medicals start from R1,500. Extra tests or reports are added only if the doctor finds they are needed during the medical. We confirm the fee when you book.

Do Class 4 holders have to send anything to SACAA between medicals?

+

Yes. Regulation 67.00.9(3) requires the holder of a Class 4 medical certificate to complete and submit a medical declaration to the designated body every year, on the form prescribed in SA-CATS 67. Contact the practice or SACAA if you are unsure which form to use.
