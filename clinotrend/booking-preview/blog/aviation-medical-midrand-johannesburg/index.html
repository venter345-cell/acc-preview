Aviation medical in Midrand and Johannesburg: what to bring, how long it takes and what it costs

Where to go at Grand Central Airport, the checklist to bring, how long to allow, what affects the cost and how to fly in.

Draft prepared 23 October 20264 min readClinotrend Health & Aviation, for review by Dr A.O. Ndhlovu

Draft, for review. This post is a draft for Dr Ndhlovu to check before it is published. See the growth plan

Most pilots and cabin crew in Gauteng look for an aviation medical that is easy to reach from home, the flight school or the airfield. Grand Central Airport in Midrand sits on the N1 between Johannesburg and Pretoria, which makes it a practical stop for both. This guide covers who can be examined at Clinotrend, what to bring, how long it takes and what goes into the cost.

Which medicals are done at Grand Central?

Dr A.O. Ndhlovu is listed by SACAA as a Regular Designated Aviation Medical Examiner, number MR 336. SACAA authorises Regular DAMEs to examine for and issue Class II and Class IV certificates.

Where to go

Clinotrend Health & Aviation is in the Main Terminal Building, Gate 2, Office TG6B, Grand Central Airport, 3 New Road, Midrand.

  • From Johannesburg and Sandton: take the N1 north, exit at New Road or Allandale and follow New Road to Grand Central Airport.
  • From Pretoria and Centurion: take the N1 south to the New Road or Allandale exit, then follow New Road to the airport.
  • By Gautrain: Midrand Gautrain Station is about 5 minutes away by e-hailing or taxi.
  • Parking: boom-gated parking at the terminal. Go to Gate 2.
  • Flying in: Grand Central is FAGC. The apron and visitor aircraft parking are directly outside the Gate 2 terminal doors, so you can combine your medical with a flight.

Pilots also come from airfields such as Lanseria, Wonderboom and Rand Airport, and patients from Randburg, Kempton Park and Germiston.

What to bring

Part 67 (regulation 67.00.7) requires you to show proof of identity, your licence and your latest medical certificate if you have one, give your medical history and sign a declaration. Bring:

  • Your South African ID, driving licence or passport
  • Any aviation licence you hold (pilot or cabin crew)
  • Your most recent aviation medical certificate, if you have one
  • Your logbook, if you are a pilot renewing
  • A list of every medicine you take, including over the counter medicines and supplements
  • Glasses, contact lenses or hearing aids, if you use them
  • Recent reports from your doctor or specialist for any medical condition
  • Your medical aid card, if applicable

You can look at the SACAA Aviation Medical Report form, CA 67-02(a).pdf) beforehand to see which history questions you will be asked.

How long does an aviation medical take?

Allow about 30 to 60 minutes, and arrive 10 minutes early to complete paperwork. The time depends on whether this is your first medical or a renewal, on your medical history and on whether you bring all your documents.

The examination covers your history, a physical examination and on-site tests: a resting ECG, vision testing, a hearing test (audiometry) and urine screening. If you meet the standards and no further reports are needed, the certificate can be issued at the end of the examination. If more information is needed, we explain the next steps before you leave.

What does an aviation medical cost?

Fees differ between examiners and are set by each practice, so compare like with like. Clinotrend does not publish fees on the website; we give you the fee when you book. What affects the total you pay:

  • The class. Class 2 and Class 4 examinations are not identical.
  • First medical or renewal. An initial medical can need more, for example SACAA lists a chest X-ray at the initial examination and afterwards only when clinically indicated.
  • Extra tests or reports. Blood tests, X-rays or specialist reports are sometimes requested because of age or medical history, and are often done and billed by another provider.
  • Medical aid. Cover differs between plans, so check with your scheme and with us before your visit.

Questions and answers

Where can I get an aviation medical near Johannesburg?

+

Dr A.O. Ndhlovu, a SACAA Regular DAME (MR 336), examines pilots and cabin crew at Clinotrend Health & Aviation, Gate 2, Grand Central Airport, Midrand, on the N1 between Johannesburg and Pretoria.

How long does an aviation medical take?

+

Allow about 30 to 60 minutes, and arrive 10 minutes early for paperwork.

How much does an aviation medical cost in Midrand?

+

Clinotrend does not publish fees on the website. The fee is given when you book and depends on the class, whether it is a first medical or a renewal, and any extra tests or reports.

Can I do a Class 1 medical at Clinotrend?

+

No. Class 1 and Class 3 medicals need a SACAA Senior DAME. Clinotrend does Class II and Class IV.

Can I fly in to Grand Central for my medical?

+

Yes. Grand Central is FAGC, and the apron and visitor aircraft parking are outside the Gate 2 terminal doors, next to the practice.
