What is an aviation medical examiner (DAME)?

In South Africa an aviation medical examiner is a Designated Aviation Medical Examiner, or DAME: a doctor designated by the Director of Civil Aviation to examine pilots, cabin crew and air traffic controllers, and to issue or defer their medical certificates on behalf of SACAA under Part 67 of the Civil Aviation Regulations.

People also look for an aviation doctor, an aero medical examiner or a flight medical examiner. In South Africa that role is the DAME.

SACAA has two categories of DAME:

  • Regular DAME: Class II and Class IV medical certificates. Dr A.O. Ndhlovu is a Regular DAME, designation MR 336.
  • Senior DAME: Class I, II, III and IV medical certificates.

SACAA publishes the names of its designated examiners. Dr A.O. Ndhlovu is listed as MR 336, at ClinoTrend Health & Aviation, Grand Central Airport, Midrand, on the SACAA list of Regular DAMEs.

Source: SACAA aviation medicine.

Which medical do you need?

ClassWho it is for
Class IIStudent pilots (SPL) and private pilots (PPL) for aeroplane, airship and helicopter, plus other licences listed by SACAA.
Class IVRecreational pilots such as microlight, glider, gyroplane and free balloon pilots, and cabin crew under the amended Part 67 regulations. See the Class 4 medical certificate page and the cabin crew medical page.

Class I (commercial and airline pilots) and Class III (air traffic controllers) need a SACAA Senior DAME. ClinoTrend does not currently issue Class 1 or Class 3. Some SACAA pages still list cabin crew under Class 2 or Class II; ClinoTrend can issue Class 2 or Class 4 for cabin crew, depending on what the airline or operator asks for. Sources: SACAA classes of medical certificates and SACAA frequently asked questions.

How the medical works

  1. 01

    Book

    Aviation medicals are by appointment only, because each one is prepared before you arrive. Book online or contact us and tell us the class you need and whether this is your first medical or a renewal. Group bookings are arranged in advance.

  2. 02

    Prepare

    Gather the documents in the checklist below. Rest well, and avoid caffeine, tobacco and high sugar meals beforehand, as they can affect urine and ECG results. Arrive 10 minutes early.

  3. 03

    Examination

    Dr A.O. Ndhlovu takes your history and examines you. A resting ECG, vision testing, a hearing test (audiometry) and urine screening are done on site. Ask the practice how long to allow when you book.

  4. 04

    Outcome

    In most cases your aviation medical is completed on the same day, depending on your case. If you meet the SACAA standards and no further reports are needed, the certificate is issued at the end of the examination. Otherwise it is deferred until the extra information arrives, and we explain what is needed.

What to bring

  • Your South African ID or passport
  • Any aviation licence you hold, and your logbook if you are renewing
  • Your most recent aviation medical certificate, if you have one
  • A list of every medicine you take, including over the counter medicines
  • Glasses, contact lenses or hearing aids, if you use them
  • Recent reports from your doctor or specialist if you have a medical condition
  • Your medical aid card, if applicable

Paying: Payment by EFT or card is required at the time of your visit. We accept most medical aids and charge medical aid rates. Self-pay patients receive a quote before treatment.

The SACAA form: CA 67-02(a)

The Aviation Medical Report is the form used on the day. Read it beforehand to see the history questions. Declare all medicines and healthcare visits it asks about; for unchanged conditions you may write "Previously reported, no change".

Aviation medical questions

What is a DAME (Designated Aviation Medical Examiner)?

+

A doctor designated by the Director of Civil Aviation to do aviation medical examinations and to issue, or defer, medical certificates on behalf of SACAA, under Part 67 of the Civil Aviation Regulations. SACAA has Regular DAMEs (Class II and Class IV) and Senior DAMEs (Class I, II, III and IV).

How do I find a SACAA aviation medical examiner near me?

+

SACAA publishes lists of Regular and Senior DAMEs on its aviation medicine page. In Midrand, Dr A.O. Ndhlovu (MR 336) is at Gate 2, Grand Central Airport, on the N1 between Johannesburg and Pretoria, and does Class II and Class IV medicals.

Can a Regular DAME do my Class 1 medical?

+

No. Class I and Class III medicals need a SACAA Senior DAME. A Regular DAME does Class II and Class IV medicals.

What forms do I need before my aviation medical?

+

The SACAA application form for a medical certificate. You can complete and sign it online on our SACAA form page before your visit; it is emailed only to the practice. The Aviation Medical Report (CA 67-02(a)) is completed on the day.

What tests are done at an aviation medical?

+

Dr A.O. Ndhlovu takes your medical history and examines you. A resting ECG, vision testing, a hearing test (audiometry) and urine screening are done on site. If your age or history means you need a test that is not done here, we tell you when you book.

Do I need a chest X-ray for an aviation medical?

+

ClinoTrend will tell you if your medical needs a chest X-ray. If your initial medical needs a chest X-ray, you can have it done at a nearby radiology practice and bring the report. These are independent practices, not run by ClinoTrend. Which class do cabin crew need?

+

Amended Part 67 lists cabin crew under Class 4. Some SACAA pages, including the aviation medicine page and a medical-requirements table in the frequently asked questions, still list Class 2 or Class II. ClinoTrend can issue Class 2 or Class 4 for cabin crew, depending on what the airline or operator asks for. Tell us which class is required when you book.

Can my own GP do a Class 4 medical?

+

SACAA allows a Class 4 applicant to be examined by a general practitioner, provided the examination follows Part 67 and the results are sent on the prescribed form for verification. Class 1, 2 and 3 applicants must see a designated aviation medical examiner.

Can I get my aviation medical done on the same day?

+

Most aviation medicals are completed on the same day, depending on your case. If you meet the SACAA standards and no further reports are needed, the certificate is issued at the end of the examination. If more reports are needed, for example from your own doctor or a specialist, the certificate follows once they are in. Medicals are by appointment only, so please book first.

How much does an aviation medical cost?

+

Aviation medicals start from R1,500. Extra tests or reports are added only if the doctor finds they are needed during the medical. We confirm the fee when you book.

Can a flight school, academy or airline book a group?

+

Yes. Group bookings for flight schools, cabin crew academies and airlines are arranged in advance. Call 011 568 7979 or email info@clinotrend-health.co.za with the number of people and the dates that suit you.

What if my certificate is about to expire?

+

Book your renewal a few weeks before the expiry date. If you need an extension, SACAA procedure AM007 says you must contact the SACAA medical department before the certificate expires.
