Vision

SACAA pilot eyesight and colour vision: what the standards say, and what happens if you do not meet them at first

What SACAA aviation medicals include for vision and colour perception, how the first colour-plate test works, and why numeric limits wait for official sign-off.

By Dr A.O. Ndhlovu, MBChB (UCT), DOHM (UP), Cert. Avmed (SMU), SACAA Designated Aviation Medical Examiner (MR 336)

Quick answer

SACAA aviation medicals include vision and colour-perception tests. Many people who wear glasses can still meet the standard. If you do not pass the first colour-plate test, that is not automatically the end: further testing may be required, and some restrictions can apply. Only the official standards and the examiner's assessment decide the outcome.

The short answer

Vision and colour perception are part of every SACAA aviation medical. At ClinoTrend, vision testing is done on site, as stated on the aviation medicals page. Form CA 67-02(a) records visual acuity (with and without correction, each eye and both eyes, for distant, intermediate and near vision), colour perception, visual fields, ocular muscle balance and other eye findings. The examiner applies the official standard to you. A blog post cannot tell you whether you will meet it.

Distance, near and intermediate vision

The visual standard sits in Part 67 and the associated technical standards. We have opened form CA 67-02(a) and the summarised medical protocols list, but we have not opened a complete official CATS 67 visual-requirement clause that we can quote as a numeric limit. This article therefore does not publish acuity figures such as a 6/6 or 6/9 standard, or any unofficial number copied from another clinic's website. Those figures, if they appear elsewhere, must be checked against the official text.

What we can say from the form itself is that both uncorrected and corrected acuity are recorded, for each eye and for both eyes, at distant, intermediate and near distances. Correction, if you use it, is part of the examination, not a surprise. If the official wording is later confirmed, it can be added with a citation and the doctor's sign-off.

Glasses and contact lenses

Many people who wear glasses or contact lenses can still meet the standard, if the corrected vision required by the official text is met. That is not a promise that every prescription will be accepted. Form CA 67-02(a), which we opened, already notes conditions for contact-lens use: monofocal, non-tinted lenses, and spare spectacles. Bring your current glasses or lenses, and a spare pair if you have one. A recent optometrist or ophthalmologist report can help, but it does not replace the aviation medical.

Colour perception

Colour perception is recorded on the same form, usually first with pseudo-isochromatic plates (often called an Ishihara test). If you do not pass that first test, that is not automatically the end of a flying career. Further testing may be required, and some restrictions can apply, depending on the official protocol and the results. This article does not paste pass marks, plate counts or clinic-specific colour-test thresholds, including figures that appear on unofficial DAME websites. Those pages are not the law.

Some unofficial pages also discuss a further colour-assessment test after the plates. We will only describe that test, and any pass mark, if the doctor confirms it from a SACAA document that has been opened, and signs the wording off.

If you do not meet the standard at first

If a finding needs more information, the usual first step is a deferral or a limitation, not a lifetime ban. Complex cases can be referred to the Aeromedical Committee. That process is explained in what happens if a medical is deferred and in the companion article what can defer or limit a SACAA medical. This page does not promise that you will still obtain any particular licence.

What happens at ClinoTrend

Vision testing is on site for Class 2 and Class 4 medicals. Class 1 eye standards, and the issue of a Class 1 certificate, sit with a Senior DAME, not with this practice. See SACAA Class 1 medical and Senior DAMEs. If you are booking a Class 2 or Class 4 medical, book online, or call 011 568 7979 or WhatsApp 078 255 1405, and bring your glasses or lenses and any recent eye reports.

Questions and answers

Can I be a pilot if I wear glasses?

+

Many people can, if the corrected official standard is met. Only the examination can apply that standard to you.

What is the SACAA eyesight requirement?

+

See Part 67 and the technical standards. This article does not publish a numeric limit until an official clause is opened and the doctor signs it off.

What if I fail the Ishihara plates?

+

Further testing may be required. This page does not predict the result.

Can I be a commercial pilot with a colour-vision deficiency?

+

Sometimes, with limits, depending on the official protocol and the test results. This page does not promise an outcome.

Should I get an eye test before my medical?

+

It can be useful to bring a recent report. It does not replace the aviation medical.

Does ClinoTrend do Class 1 eye standards?

+

Class 1 is issued only by a Senior DAME. We do Class 2 and Class 4 vision testing here.
